### Introduction

Everyone knows, everyone has at some point come across all sorts of saws, take just the simplest ones, hand saws, then electric and petrol ones, all the way up to the aristocracy among saws, which intricately cut precious stones that later adorn the ears, the wrists and the grooves between the wrinkled or firm breasts of all sorts of ladies, because vain ones as well as dignified or deranged ones. Besides, a certain peculiar part of gentlemen by no means scorns such jewels either. But once there was a certain saw, quite exceptional, and surely she is still somewhere out there, abandoned, the teeth of her chain covered with rust. This saw even has a name – she is called Mańka, she has a temper – she does what she wants – a man, once he grabs her in his paws, fulfils all her whims. Not to drag out this introduction, I will only say that this little story takes place in some godforsaken little town in eastern Poland in the first years of the twentieth century. And Mańka is dependent on petrol and on oil.

### Before

Mańka lies idle on the shelf and remembers the beautiful old days, when with the teeth of her chain she melted into the trunks of dried-up plum trees, apple trees, and the chips gave off a delightful scent. The Man would carry her out of the orchard with reverence, clean her, sharpen every tooth of the chain with a file, give her petrol to drink. She lay proud in the place of honour in the shed, swaddled in a rag that protected her from the damp, while the man's guests wolfed down roast sausage, pieces of chicken, washed it down with vodka and, glancing at her, nodded their heads in admiration of her dancing capers in the orchard.

### Now

Now all the teeth of the chain, except one, are covered with a layer of red rust. That one tooth sometimes flashes with a silver glint, and when the Man walks past with a heavy step, the wooden floor resonates and Mańka gently sways her body, flashes the healthy eye of her chain and shouts – I'm here. Give me petrol and oil. I'm waiting. You won't regret it. Together we can do heroic deeds, just get me into your paws, I'm waiting. The Man suddenly wakes from his lethargy, his eyes shine, his sick legs gain vigour, he runs about nimbly, full of zeal. Mańka is in seventh heaven. Human hands clean her rusty teeth with a rag soaked in solvent. With delight she feels the petrol being poured in, and the oil squeezes in, tickles, caresses all her little cogs, idle for months. When at last her starved maw begins to move with dozens of gleaming chain teeth, Mańka gets an incredible kick – it's a full orgasm, multiplied by spasms. Now the Man is as if absent – only sometimes can his hands be seen, clenched on the handle of the saw. Mańka, inflamed, sweeps her gaze over the room where the Man has carried her. All around, tasty morsels. She can't decide where to begin the feast. I think I'll start with the fucking table – she growls, all fired up. She sinks her teeth into one of the table's legs. First cut, second cut – the table tilts, wobbles. Mańka goes for the second leg. A moment later the tabletop, stripped of its legs, rests on the floor. The chips sway provocatively, a pile of boards heaps up on the floor, and Mańka, dancing, makes short work of the sideboard, grinds her teeth when she hits the metal hinges, but after a moment's rest, with quick cuts, turns the chairs and shelves into irregular chunks of wood. A child huddled in the corner sobs pitifully. A woman comes into the room, snatches the child into her arms and, slamming the door, leaves the battlefield. Put back on the shelf in the shed, Mańka lies there fulfilled, savouring the oak chips lodged in the gaps between her blunted teeth, and looks at the Man, who sits in an old armchair and dozes.

### Afterwards

Years later Mańka rests on the same shelf, shackled by a tangle of cobwebs, dirty, dejected and forgotten. Even her one healthy tooth is covered with a patina of rust. She shakes herself for a moment, because the Man comes into the shed dragging his feet, making the wooden floor creak and spring, so that the shelf sways, spreading clouds of dust. The Man flops heavily onto a chair and pulls a thick rope out of his bag. And at that moment, inside Mańka, the cogs not yet rusted begin to quiver, the plastic cover falls off and the metal parts, reflecting the light of the Indian summer in distinct streaks and glints, form on the grey plaster of the wall this very picture – the Man has a noose of thick rope around his neck and sits on the chair, starts Mańka, and she lovingly cuts the legs out from under the chair, then the Man sways for a moment and falls, and his head hangs just above the floor. The Man lights a cigarette, finishes off the rest of the vodka, looks at the fading picture and hurls the bottle against the wall, yelling.

“Piss off, you slut, I'd sooner throw you into a bucket of dirty water than hang myself.” A white dove flies into the shed, lands on the cluttered floor and shuffles about, bewildered.
