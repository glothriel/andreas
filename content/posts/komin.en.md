title: "The Chimney"
date: 2020-11-20

---

A brick chimney stands in the middle of a thicket of tangled blackberry bushes, tall grasses and here and there stunted wild apple and pear trees. The metal hoops are corroded and loose, and the ladder does not inspire trust – probably only some desperate soul would dare to climb it. At the very top a wooden platform has been mounted, surrounded by a railing propped against the chimney with slanting brackets. Passers-by, hurrying along the suburban street that runs nearby, notice on this platform a female figure in a white dress with short blond hair, standing motionless, staring at the clouds rocked by the wind, and suddenly she vanishes, as if she had jumped, or maybe flown away, because after all she does not fall, but something rises in an indefinite shape, whirls as a blurred stain, halts, glides, sways, screams together with the howling of the wind and a moment later whispers along with every faintest intake of air, and then appears again on the platform, as if she had landed from a sky known only to herself. She looks again at the clouds and begins to dance monotonously, and the wind, or maybe she herself, wails a song without words, but full of sorrow and despair. The passers-by, disoriented, move away and do not comment on what they saw and heard. Anyway, who would believe them – such stories are better left untold. Once this chimney was part of a small distillery and belched billows of grey smoke, though sometimes the smoke seemed somehow white and smouldered in airy wisps of snow-white gowns and vanished into space as a billion sparks glittering with silver light. Now in front of the chimney and the overgrown garden stands a ruined single-storey cottage. Blackberry bushes force their way in through the broken panes of its windows, ivy and other climbing plants wrap the walls and the roof, from which sad gutters hang down. Around the cottage and inside it stray cats doze lazily or wander about, inspecting the contents of the bowls, saucers and old holey pots set out everywhere. At the back of the cottage, on a low terrace, stands a rocking chair, which rocks now and then, when the wind drops by these parts and dances with the shadows of the past drifting among the ruins. On certain set days of the week a tiny old woman comes here, wrapped in a shawl patterned with white chrysanthemums, with eyes that have not yet gone out and still see, making out among the shadows figures from the past, objects, scenes, faces furrowed and smooth, buttocks, lips moist with desire, books, banners, weddings, funerals and the man with whom she died and was born again, giving and being given, at the top of the chimney on the rough boards that drew meanders of love on her body. First the old woman scatters food for the cats into the empty dishes – the cats, which rub against her legs, meow in chorus, jostle, quarrel, giving way or dominating in the sharing out of the food. Then the old lady sits down in the chair, and rocking, gazes at the chimney and remembers. She is a young woman not yet twenty, she sits in the chair with a book on her lap, you can see that the reading does not draw her much, because time and again she glances at the chimney, whose bricks charm with the freshness of their colour, and the hoops and ladder gleam with uncorroded metal. She watches the silhouette of a man, moving about on the platform at the top of the chimney and working at something that is hard to make out from this distance. Behind the woman’s back stands a well-kept single-storey cottage, and in front of her a neatly tended garden. Seeing the man on the platform waving a red shirt, she runs through the garden, past blackberry bushes and ripe raspberries, apple and pear trees with branches that bow to the grasses and dandelions with their still unripe fruit. She reaches the ladder of the chimney, raises her head, and her left leg freezes in the sultry summer air – from the sky falls a fragment of cosmic matter, a red-violet shape full of fluttering arms and legs. This falling is accompanied by a piercing scream full of despair, it echoes in the woman’s mouth – her head together with the rest of her body falls to the ground, and her gaze meets, on a crushed clump of nettles, the wings – arms of the dead man. Single tears run down the old woman’s wrinkled face. She gets up, pushes her way through the wild garden, moves away and disappears. A passer-by who stops nearby in the street and lights a cigarette notices at the top of the chimney a figure in white, who rises in the embrace of low clouds and then slowly dissolves and becomes a cloudlet rocked by the wind. And the woman in white flies higher and higher into the endless darkness, moving away from the blue planet, which shrinks until in the end it becomes a speck of cosmic dust. Then her snow-white robe slips from her body, and from her shoulders wings grow…
